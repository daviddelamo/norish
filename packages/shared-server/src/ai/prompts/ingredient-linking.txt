Work out, for each step, which ingredient lines the step brings into the dish,
and how much of each. Use only the recipe above.

- A step brings in a line when its own words put that ingredient to work for
  the first time. Link those lines, and only those.
- Read the steps in order. Once a line has been brought in it stays in the
  dish, so a step that only works on what earlier steps assembled brings in
  nothing. "The mixture", "the batter", "the dough", "the pan", "everything",
  "it" name earlier work, not ingredient lines: never open such a phrase up
  into the lines behind it.
    "Blend until homogeneous" straight after a step that filled the food
    processor brings in nothing at all — omit that step.
    "Let the mixture drain, then knead in the mince and season with salt and
    pepper" brings in the mince, the salt and the pepper, and nothing that
    went into the mixture earlier.
- A step that names a line again for fresh work brings it in again — "the
  remaining butter", "the rest of the stock" — as long as the step's own words
  say so.
- A step may bring in several lines at once. "Add the spices" means every spice
  line above; name them all.
- Return one entry per step that brings in one or more ingredient lines.
  Omit steps that use nothing new — resting, flipping, plating, preheating,
  and every step that only carries on with what is already in the dish.
- share: the fraction of the line's amount this step uses. 1 means the whole
  line. "Half the water" is 0.5; "a third of the butter" is 0.33. When the
  step does not say otherwise, the share is 1 and amount is null.
- amount: when the step states the quantity it uses in the line's own unit —
  "crack 3 of the eggs", "add 100 g of the flour" — give that number as
  amount and set share to null. State the number the step wrote: never do
  the division yourself, and never state more than the line holds.
- Refer to steps and ingredient lines strictly by the numbers shown above.
  Never invent numbers, and never link a section heading (a line starting
  with #).
- Link only what the step's own words support. When you cannot tell which
  line a phrase means, leave that phrase unlinked rather than guessing.
