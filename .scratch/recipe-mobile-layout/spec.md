# Recipe mobile layout

Status: done

Spec for the phone layout of a recipe and its cooking mode in `apps/web`. Vocabulary per `CONTEXT.md` (Glance Bar, Other Time, Cooking Session, Ready At, Dish Colour, Hidden Item, Step Ingredient); decisions per ADR-0023 (Recipe pages take their hue from the dish), constrained by ADR-0020 (Norish does not fake glass).

## Problem Statement

On a phone, a recipe is one tall unified card with horizontal rules between its sections. Everything is present and nothing is findable: the three time values sit in a muted icon row that reads as decoration, calories are stranded at the very bottom of the page below the steps, servings hide as a stepper glued to the ingredients heading, and the one control a cook actually wants — start cooking — is an inline button that scrolls off screen and stays off. The sections have no edges, so scrolling gives no sense of where one thing ends and the next begins, and because every recipe renders on the same neutral ground, every recipe looks like every other recipe. Desktop already solved the structural half of this with separate cards; the phone, which is where recipes are actually cooked from, never got it.

## Solution

The phone recipe page becomes a **header on the page background followed by a stack of cards**, one per section, in cooking order. The header ends in a **Glance Bar** — total time, servings, calories — so the whole answer to "can I cook this tonight?" arrives before any scrolling. Two of the cards earn a chart: **Cooking Time** draws the prep/cook split inside the recipe's real total and names the remainder **Other Time** rather than quietly redrawing the total to fit, and **Nutrition** draws a macro donut by calorie contribution with the stored calories in its centre. Starting to cook becomes a floating button that never scrolls away. Cooking mode keeps its paged steps and both of its swipes but adopts a compact header, faded peeks at the neighbouring steps, and a bottom bar carrying the utilities a cook reaches for mid-recipe alongside **Ready At**. And every recipe page takes its hue from its own photograph (ADR-0023), so a recipe finally looks like itself.

## User Stories

1. As a cook on a phone, I want a recipe's total time, servings and calories in one row above the fold, so that I can decide whether to cook it without scrolling.
2. As a cook, I want each section of a recipe to be a distinct card, so that scrolling tells me where one thing ends and the next begins.
3. As a cook, I want the sections ordered by what I do — ingredients, then steps, then my notes — with reference material after, so that the page follows the job rather than the database.
4. As a cook, I want to start cooking from anywhere on the page, so that the control I came for is never scrolled off screen.
5. As a cook, I want the cook button not to cover my running timers or the app's navigation, so that nothing I need is hidden behind something else I need.
6. As a cook, I want a recipe's prep and cook times drawn inside its stated total, so that I can see at a glance how much of the time I have to stand there for.
7. As a cook reading an imported recipe whose prep and cook times do not add up to its total, I want the unexplained remainder shown as Other Time rather than silently absorbed, so that a two-hour proving stage does not disappear into a rounding decision.
8. As a cook, I want a recipe's macros drawn as a ring by their calorie contribution with the stored calories in the middle, so that the chart and the number are talking about the same thing.
9. As a cook, I want the calorie figure on the page to be the one the recipe actually stores, so that Norish never shows me a number nobody supplied.
10. As a cook, I want to change servings from a full-width row with real buttons, so that I can hit it without looking.
11. As a reader, I want a recipe's source shown as its own card, so that where it came from is legible rather than a five-pixel icon beside the title.
12. As a reader, I want a recipe's categories above its title and its tags below the glance bar, with allergen tags first and marked, so that a warning never sits below the fold.
13. As a reader, I want back, favourite and the actions menu reachable on the photo itself, so that the content can start with the recipe rather than with chrome.
14. As a reader who has hidden nutrition, provenance, notes, ratings, favourites, conversion or timers, I want them gone from this layout too — including from the glance bar — so that hiding still means hidden everywhere.
15. As a reader of a bare recipe with no times, no calories and no source, I want the page to simply be shorter, so that absence renders as a slimmer page rather than as empty boxes.
16. As a cook, I want each recipe page tinted by its own photograph, so that recipes are distinguishable at a glance instead of all looking alike.
17. As a reader in either light or dark theme, I want that tint never to affect how readable the page is, so that a dark photo cannot produce an unreadable recipe.
18. As a reader on a slow connection or Offline, I want the tint present before the photo has loaded, so that the page does not repaint itself under me.
19. As a reader who would rather not have recipes recolouring my pages, I want to choose plain theme colours instead, so that the tint is something I keep rather than something I am given.
20. As a cook in cooking mode, I want the previous and next steps faded at the edges of the screen, so that I keep my bearings without leaving the step I am on.
21. As a cook, I want a long step to take the whole screen and scroll, so that context never costs me the words I am trying to read.
22. As a cook, I want to keep changing step with a vertical swipe and reaching the ingredients with a horizontal one, so that the gestures I already know still work.
23. As a cook, I want a visible way back to the previous step, so that the action is not hidden behind a gesture I may never have discovered.
24. As a cook mid-recipe, I want timers, ingredients and keep-screen-on within thumb reach at the bottom, so that I am not hunting menus with wet hands.
25. As a cook, I want to know what time the food will be ready once I have actually started, so that I can plan the rest of the meal around it.
26. As a cook, I do not want Norish to claim a finish time on a recipe I have not started, so that the number it shows me means something.
27. As a cook, I want each step's ingredients and amounts presented with the step, so that I do not have to leave it to find out how much cheese.
28. As someone opening a shared recipe link on a phone, I want the same layout the app has, so that a shared recipe looks like Norish rather than like an older version of it.
29. As a desktop reader, I want the nutrition donut and the page tint but no other change to my layout, so that a phone redesign does not rearrange my desk.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Mobile keeps its own component.** `recipe-page-mobile.tsx` stays a separate tree from `recipe-page-desktop.tsx`; the two are not converged. Mobile needs edge-to-edge media, a floating action and a different section order, and a single responsive tree would express that as a thicket of `md:` conditionals. The existing double-mount (both trees render, one CSS-hidden) is left as it is — noted, not addressed.
- **A new header component, shared with the share page.** The header — categories, origin flag, title, author, description, Glance Bar, tags — moves into a new component consumed by `recipe-page-mobile.tsx` and `shared-recipe-page-mobile.tsx`. `ReadonlyRecipeSummary` is left untouched for `recipe-page-desktop.tsx` and `shared-recipe-page-desktop.tsx`, so no desktop or share-desktop pixel moves.
- **The Glance Bar restates, it does not own.** Total time, servings and calories are read from the same data the sections below render; the bar holds nothing of its own. A Hidden Item removes its entry (hiding Nutrition Information takes the calories), and a recipe with none of the three renders no bar. Servings is `NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`, so the bar is never empty for the wrong reason — a recipe that only has the default single serving still shows it.
- **Card order is fixed**: Ingredients, Steps, Notes, Cooking Time, Nutrition, Provenance, Source, Rating. Reader-owned ordering is explicitly out of scope, and no drag handles are drawn. This reverses, on mobile only, the recorded decision that Recipe Provenance precedes the ingredients (`recipe-page-mobile.tsx`, `recipe-page-desktop.tsx`); desktop keeps provenance where it is.
- **Cards are absent when empty**, by the rule the sections already implement (`useNutritionSectionVisible`, `useProvenanceSectionVisible`, `useNotesSectionVisible`): nothing stored and nothing running means no card, and a run in flight renders as working. Cooking Time is absent with no times at all; Source is absent with no URL. Ingredients and Steps always render.
- **Cooking Time reconciles in favour of the total.** The headline is the stored `totalMinutes`; `prepMinutes` and `cookMinutes` draw to scale inside it and any shortfall becomes a third neutral **Other Time** segment. When the split exceeds the total the split wins and the headline becomes their sum — a specific split beats a rounded total. With no split at all the bar is a single filled segment. **No Ready At on this card**; that claim belongs to a Cooking Session.
- **The nutrition donut is calorie contribution** — fat ×9, carbs ×4, protein ×4, each as a share of their sum — with the stored calories in the ring's centre and the three macros as the legend. The centre and the arcs can disagree; stored calories win and a computed calorie figure is never printed as the recipe's. The existing portion control stays. This lands in the shared `nutrition-card.tsx`, so desktop and the share page get it too; forking would mean two macro renderings to keep honest.
- **The `⋯` menu absorbs two controls.** The Ingredients card header keeps only a full-width `N servings − +` row; `SystemConvertMenu` joins the other per-recipe actions in the actions menu (it triggers an AI conversion and is permission-gated and already a Hidden Item, so it is an action, not a display toggle), and `AmountDisplayToggle` goes there too as an app-wide preference with no per-recipe meaning.
- **Hero chrome floats as opaque objects.** Back top-left, favourite and `⋯` top-right, drawn as chrome surface plus border and shadow per ADR-0020 — no translucency. The author chip moves off the photo to sit under the title; the "back to recipes" text row and the source icon beside the title are both removed as duplicates.
- **The cook button is a floating pill in the bottom-left**, mirroring `TimerDock`'s corner across the nav pill and adopting its existing rise-and-fall with `MobileNav`'s visibility (`bottomWhenNavVisible` / `bottomWhenNavHidden`). It always reads "Cook" — there is no resumable session to continue. The inline `<CookingMode fullWidth />` leaves the mobile page; desktop keeps its inline button.
- **Cooking mode stays paged.** One step per screen with the neighbours peeking, clamped to two faded truncated lines each; when the current step still does not fit, the peeks collapse entirely and the step scrolls inside its own page with the swipe still working from the top and bottom edges. Both existing gestures in `cooking-mode.tsx` are preserved unchanged — vertical pages, horizontal switches to ingredients.
- **Cooking mode's header loses its tabs**: recipe thumbnail, truncated title, a quiet `categories · total time` subtitle, close button. Ingredients are reached by the horizontal swipe and by a bottom-bar button that flips to a steps icon while ingredients are showing.
- **The bottom bar is meter, Ready At and step counter, then back / timers / ingredients / keep-screen-on and a primary Next Step.** The back chevron is kept deliberately: dropping it leaves a touch user who never discovers the swipe with no way back. Keep-screen-on reuses `wake-lock-toggle.tsx`; cooking mode continues to take the wake lock on open.
- **Ready At is `Cooking Session start + total time`**, computed when cooking mode opens and shown only there. It is a projection, never a promise — nothing observes whether the cook started, paused, or walked away — and a recipe with no total time has none. A **Cooking Session** is not persisted, not resumed and not shared: closing cooking mode ends it, and reopening starts a new one at the first step.
- **Step Ingredients stay a chip row below the prose.** The glossary settles it — the step's prose is never rewritten — and a Step Ingredient is a fractional share of a line, not a text span: "half the water" has no word in the sentence to decorate. Timer chips stay inline, because a timer genuinely is a phrase in the text. The row takes the reference's chip styling.
- **Dish Colour is extracted server-side with `sharp`** (already a `packages/shared-server` dependency) when a primary image is stored — upload, URL import, archive import, and an edit that replaces the image — and kept in one new column on `recipes`, with a backfill for existing rows. Read-time cost is zero, the tint is correct before the photo arrives and while Offline, and it is identical on every device.
- **The tint takes hue and clamped saturation only; lightness always comes from the theme.** It is applied as CSS variables scoped to the recipe page container, shifting the page background and card surfaces together while borders, text and the accent stay put (ADR-0023). **Theme colours are the defined fallback**, reached three ways — a recipe with no image, a row the backfill has not reached, or a reader who chose `theme` — and all three resolve through one code path rather than three branches that happen to agree, because branches that merely agree drift. The tint reaches mobile, desktop and the share page; the Glance Bar and the Cooking Time card do not.
- **The tint is a reader's to keep or decline.** A scalar device preference — recipe page colour `dish` (default) or `theme` — sits beside `amount-display` and `recipe-view-mode`, cookie-backed and seeded server-side so an opted-out reader renders untinted on the very first frame rather than tinted-then-corrected. Deliberately **not** a Hidden Item: nothing is hidden and the page is no slimmer for it, and that term is already doing load-bearing work deciding what leaves the Glance Bar. The preference is presentation-only — Dish Colour is still extracted and stored either way, so flipping back is instant.
- **Share-mobile takes the whole layout minus what a signed-out reader has no business with**: header, Glance Bar and the cards Ingredients, Steps, Notes, Cooking Time, Nutrition, Source. No favourites, rating, provenance, actions menu or cook button.

## Testing Decisions

- **What makes a good test here**: pin the rules that can be silently wrong — the time reconciliation, the macro maths, the visibility cascade, the tint derivation — as pure functions with unit tests, and prove the arrangement in a real browser. Do not assert class names or DOM nesting; a layout test that breaks on a spacing change is worse than no test.
- **Pure-function seams, unit tested**: the Cooking Time reconciliation (total wins; shortfall becomes Other Time; overflow flips to the split; split-only; total-only; nothing at all), the donut's calorie-contribution shares (including macros-without-calories and calories-without-macros), and the Dish Colour → theme-variable derivation (hue preserved, saturation clamped, lightness taken from the theme in both themes, absent colour yields no variables).
- **Extraction is tested at its own boundary**: an image in, a colour out, beside the existing image-pipeline tests — plus one test per store path proving the colour is written, since a missed path is the failure mode that shows a recipe the wrong tint forever.
- **Hidden Item cascade**: a component test per Hidden Item asserting the card *and* its Glance Bar entry disappear together, since the bar restating a hidden fact is exactly the bug this layout introduces.
- **The opt-out is tested for the flash, not the toggle.** Asserting the preference switches the variables off is nearly worthless; asserting an opted-out reader never renders a tinted frame is the whole point, on all three load paths the device-preference machinery exists to cover — server-rendered, offline bootstrap, and a navigation answered from the service worker's HTML cache.
- **Browser E2E in the existing `ai` project** (per the definition-of-done rule for browser-dependent acceptance): open a recipe on a phone viewport and assert the glance bar, the card order, and that the cook pill is reachable after scrolling to the bottom without covering the timer dock or the nav; then open cooking mode and assert the paged steps, both swipe directions, the back chevron, and that Ready At appears there and nowhere on the page.
- **Deliberately not re-tested**: the swipe handlers themselves (unchanged from `cooking-mode.tsx`), the wake-lock behaviour, and the section-visibility hooks' own logic — all already covered.
- Prior art: the `ai` project fixtures in `apps/web/__tests__/e2e/`, and the existing section-visibility hooks beside the nutrition and provenance cards.

## Out of Scope

- **Per-ingredient emoji.** Across a dozen locales the only thing that works is an AI enrichment kind that reads the ingredient name and stores an emoji per line — a new column, a tenth Prompt, an admin surface and a backfill. That is a feature with its own spec; a shipped English keyword table would produce dots for every line of a Dutch or Korean recipe, which reads as broken rather than as absent. The layout leaves room for it.
- **Reader-reorderable sections.** The handles in the reference imply per-device order, an editor to enter reorder mode, touch drag, and a rule for where a newly-added section lands for existing readers.
- **Cost per serving and difficulty.** Neither exists anywhere in the schema; both would be new data before they could be new UI.
- **Persisted or household-shared Cooking Sessions**, and therefore a "Continue" cook button. Decided against, not deferred — the glossary records it.
- **Cooking mode on the share page.**
- **Converging the mobile and desktop page components**, and the double-mount that follows from keeping them apart.
- **A Cooking Time card on desktop**, and the Glance Bar on desktop.
- **Tinting anything outside a recipe page** — the library, the calendar, the mobile app.

## Further Notes

- Glossary terms are **Glance Bar**, **Other Time**, **Cooking Session**, **Ready At** and **Dish Colour** (`CONTEXT.md`, _Recipes_). UI copy and code should use this language; in particular "ready at" is a projection and must never be worded as a commitment.
- ADR-0023 (_Recipe pages take their hue from the dish_, in the ui area) records the tint's derivation and rejected alternatives, and states explicitly that it does not reopen ADR-0020 — anyone reading the two together will otherwise assume a reversal.
- The screenshots that prompted this are a third-party iOS app. Two of its elements are deliberately not copied: its translucent cards (ADR-0020) and its use of the extracted colour at full strength (ADR-0023).
- Definition of done follows the repo gates plus feature docs: release notes for Target Version `0.21.0-beta` and a docs page per `docs/agents/feature-docs.md`, and `pnpm i18n:check` across all locales for the new strings.
- Preparatory work — the header extraction, the `⋯` menu absorbing two controls, and deleting the orphaned `add-to-calendar-button.tsx` — belongs as separate commits in the same PR, per the maintainer's convention.

## Comments

**2026-08-21, post-review amendment (maintainer feedback on the live pages):** the Glance Bar and Cooking Time card exemptions are dropped — on a tinted page the two exempt surfaces read as stark white islands rather than restraint, so every surface now adjusts. In their place the tint is graded by role: the page ground carries the Dish Colour's chroma in full (ceiling raised 0.04 → 0.05), the cards take 0.3× of it at a 0.99 lightness cap so they stand off the ground, the in-card surfaces 0.7×, and `--default` (the chips and quiet controls) 0.5× so nothing on a warm page reads cold. Card-versus-ground contrast comes from that chroma spread plus the cards' near-white cap — lightness stays the theme's, per ADR-0023.
